Most dropout-prediction studies on the public UCI Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success dataset (N=4,424) drop the third outcome, Enrolled, or treat it as an unknown fate. The label is assigned at the end of the normal course duration, so Enrolled students are delayed. They neither graduated on time nor left. We compare eight classifiers in a binary setting and in the three-class setting at three prediction points (enrolment, first semester, second semester), using repeated cross-validation, corrected t-tests and nested tuning. Most models are statistically tied in both settings. In the three-class setting, macro F1 falls to 0.712, mainly because of the delayed class (F1 0.483, against 0.794 for Dropout and 0.859 for Graduate); dropout itself is still ranked well (AUC 0.926) with calibrated probabilities. Used as an alert list after one semester, the top 20% of students by predicted risk include 57% of eventual dropouts, and 92% of the students on that list drop out. The model misses more dropouts where dropout is rare. With both semesters and a 20% list, recall is 0.37 for scholarship holders against 0.61 for others; group-specific thresholds raise it to 0.57 while overall precision moves from 0.94 to 0.93. Across degree programmes the model was not trained on, within-programme ranking is preserved but probabilities need recalibration. Results at enrolment depend on two fee-status variables whose recording time is undocumented; without them the dropout AUC at enrolment falls from 0.822 to 0.770. All numbers are generated from a public repository.
