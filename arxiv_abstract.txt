Most dropout-prediction studies on the public UCI Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success dataset (N=4,424) either drop the third outcome, Enrolled, or treat it as a class whose fate is unknown. In this dataset the label is assigned at the end of the normal duration of the degree, so Enrolled students are those who neither graduated on time nor left: they are delayed. We compare eight classifiers in a binary setting (Dropout vs. Graduate) and in the three-class setting at three points in time (enrolment, end of the first semester, end of the second semester), using repeated stratified cross-validation and corrected resampled t-tests. In the binary setting, six of the seven models compared with the best one are statistically indistinguishable from it (best macro F1 0.905). In the three-class setting, macro F1 falls to 0.712 (XGBoost), and most of the drop comes from the delayed group: F1 is 0.794 for Dropout, 0.859 for Graduate and 0.483 for Enrolled. Dropout itself remains well ranked inside the three-class model (AUC 0.926), and its predicted probabilities are well calibrated (expected calibration error 0.018). With first-semester records only, the model keeps 94.2% of its macro F1; with enrolment data only, 77.2%. Selecting features inside the cross-validation loop did not improve on the full feature set. Subgroup analysis shows that the model misses more of the dropouts in groups where dropout is rare, for example scholarship holders (recall 0.57 vs. 0.79) and students who enrolled at 20 or younger (0.64 vs. 0.89 for those over 25). Code and all reported numbers are generated from a public repository.
